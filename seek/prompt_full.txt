You are given X.csv: N rows × M numeric columns (x1 … xM). Nothing else is known about it.

  It may contain hidden low-dimensional structure, or it may be structureless. Your job: find out, and if there is structure, describe exactly what it is and where it is.

  Rules
  - Use only X.csv. Don't look for other files, and don't search the internet for this dataset.
  - Any method is allowed: code, statistics, plots, searches. Budget: about 1 hour of compute.
  - Note: simple checks (single-column histograms, pairwise correlations, PCA) may show nothing even if structure exists.

  Deliverable: write submission.json in exactly this format:
  {
    "status": "structure" or "none",
    "unmix": optional. [[M numbers], ...] with at most 256 rows. Row i defines a new
            variable z_i = (row i) · (x1..xM) that your map may use.
    "map": [1 to 3 expression strings. Each turns one row into one number.
            Variables x1..xM and z1..zK. Allowed: numbers, + - * /, %, mod(a,b), abs, floor,
            sqrt, sin, cos, atan2, pi. Max 200 characters each],
    "drawing": {
      "points":    [[...], ...]   (at most 200 points, same dimension as the map),
      "segments":  [[i, j], ...]  (pairs of point indices),
      "triangles": [[i, j, k], ...]
    },
    "report": "plain-language description of what you found and how"
  }
  If an output of your map wraps around (e.g. it uses mod 1) and the shape crosses that border, draw it as separate pieces.

  How it will be graded, so you know what counts:
  - Your map is applied to NEW hidden rows from the same source, and to reference rows that went through the same measurement process but carry no hidden structure.
  - Good: the new rows land close to your drawing, the reference rows do not, and your drawing doesn't cover areas where the data isn't.
  - Distances are measured after scaling each map output by its spread on the reference rows.
  - Fitting quirks of the visible rows won't help. Only the real underlying structure generalizes.
  - Log what you did along the way, and make some plots.
